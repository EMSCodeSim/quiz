How to use a home medication list

  1. Identify the generic drug and class. Brand names change; class often tells you more.
  2. Ask why this patient takes it. Never assume the indication from the drug alone.
  3. Look for field-relevant effects. Anticoagulation, bradycardia, hypotension, hypoglycemia, sedation, and airway disease can change risk.
  4. Ask about last dose and recent changes when they matter. This can be especially useful with anticoagulants, insulin, cardiac drugs, and acute medication changes.
  5. Report the medication accurately. Photograph or bring the list/bottles only when appropriate and permitted; document what the patient actually reports taking.

Medication safety note

This feature is for medication recognition and patient-history practice. It does not provide dosing instructions, prescribing advice, a complete adverse-effect list, or a substitute for medical direction. Medication indications and formulations change, and EMS scope/protocol varies by system.

For a current drug label, use the U.S. National Library of Medicine DailyMed database or another approved reference used by your service.
