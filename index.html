Know what the exam actually weights

Current EMT content is assessment-heavy.

The National Registry’s updated EMT examination reorganized the old topic buckets into five practice-centered domains. Primary Assessment is the largest portion of the exam.

Scene Size-Up & Safety
15–19%

Hazards, resources, mechanism/nature of illness, initial safety decisions.

Primary Assessment
39–43%

General impression, life threats, airway, breathing, circulation and priority.

Secondary Assessment
5–9%

Focused history, physical exam and relevant assessment findings.

Treatment & Transport
20–24%

Interventions, reassessment, destination and transport decisions.

Operations
10–14%

EMS systems, communication, safety, incidents and operations.

Source: National Registry certification examination content, current as reviewed August 10, 2026. Pediatric care is integrated throughout the domains. View the official NREMT examination content.

How to use the site

Do the five

Answer without notes. Treat each item like a patient-care priority question, not a trivia question.

Read every rationale

Even a correct guess is a weak answer if you cannot explain why it is the best next action.

Follow the weak area

The quiz stores missed domains on your device and points you toward the topic that deserves the next review.

Clinical accuracy standard

Daily EMS Quiz is an independent education site, not a protocol or medical-control system. Questions are written around nationally published EMS examination blueprints and broadly accepted emergency-care priorities. When medication doses, scopes, or procedures vary by system, your local protocol wins.

Resuscitation content should be checked against the current 2025 AHA CPR & ECC guidance, and ACS content against the 2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI guideline.

Daily EMS Quiz FAQ

Is Daily EMS Quiz free?

Yes. The daily EMT and paramedic quizzes and the core study tools are free and do not require an account.

Is this an official NREMT practice test?

No. Daily EMS Quiz is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Registry. The site uses the public NREMT examination blueprint to organize study topics.

Why only five questions a day?

The daily set is meant to be easy to repeat. You can do another practice set after submitting if you want more, but the default habit stays short.

Does this replace local EMS protocols?

No. Medication doses, scope of practice, destination rules, and procedures may vary. Follow your education program, medical direction, and local/state protocols.
